# FoundryFabric fork of pgplex/pgparser

Branch `ff` is upstream `v0.2.0` plus the patches below. Tags are
`v0.2.0-ff.N`. The module path stays `github.com/pgplex/pgparser`, so a
consumer selects this fork with a `replace` directive and drops it once
upstream ships the fix.

| AND/OR chains arrive flat, as PostgreSQL 16's `makeAndExpr`/`makeOrExpr` build them: when the LEFT operand is already the same connective its argument list is extended, so `a AND b AND c` and `(a AND b) AND c` are one AND of three while `a AND (b AND c)` keeps its inner AND, and NOT is never merged (it was always a two-argument BoolExpr, nesting one level per term). Each extension is an append, so a 10,000-term chain is one BoolExpr built without recursion. More node kinds carry PostgreSQL 16's location (a byte offset): `BoolExpr` at its AND/OR, at NOT, at `x NOT IN`'s NOT, at IS for IS NOT DOCUMENT/NORMALIZED and at the operand for IS NOT JSON; `NullTest` and `BooleanTest` at IS, ISNULL or NOTNULL (`NullTest.Argisrow` stays unset: PostgreSQL sets it during analysis); `SubLink` at IN/NOT/the operator, or at its first token for `(SELECT ...)`, EXISTS and ARRAY; `CaseExpr` at CASE; `A_ArrayExpr` at ARRAY (the outermost) or `[`; `RowExpr` at ROW or `(`; `CoalesceExpr`, `MinMaxExpr`, `GroupingFunc`, `SQLValueFunction`, `NamedArgExpr` and `SetToDefault` at their first token; `CollateClause` (in an expression) at COLLATE; `JsonIsPredicate` at its operand; `TypeName` at its first token wherever it comes from `Typename`, a typed literal (`date 'x'`, `pg_catalog.text 'x'`, `interval(2) 'x'`) or a function's `t.c%TYPE` (after SETOF for `SETOF t.c%TYPE`); a TypeName built elsewhere (XMLSERIALIZE's target type, some DDL) may still be `-1`. Before, `BoolExpr` built for AND/OR/NOT, `NullTest` and `BooleanTest` were `0`; the NOT built for NOT IN and IS NOT DOCUMENT/NORMALIZED/JSON, and the rest, were `-1`. `nodes.ExprLocation(n) int` is where a raw expression starts, following PostgreSQL 16's exprLocation for raw nodes: a token node at its own location; an operator, connective, null/boolean test, sublink, COLLATE, named argument, cast (its type name included) or function call at the leftmost known of its own location and its first operand's (so `x AT TIME ZONE z`, being `timezone(z, x)`, is at AT, as in PostgreSQL); a subscript or field selection at its operand; a list at its first located member. A nil node, typed or not, is absent. Where the walk meets a kind with no rule (`XmlExpr`, the JSON constructors, ...) the result is `-1`, never the offset of an operator to its right; PostgreSQL knows a start there. `XmlExpr` is not located | `parser/gram.y`, `parser/parser.go` (generated), `nodes/exprlocation.go` | `TestBoolExprShapeIsPostgreSQLs`, `TestLongAndChainIsOneBoolExpr`, `TestExprNodeLocationsArePostgreSQLs`, `TestExprLocationIsWhereTheExpressionStarts`, `TestExprLocationRules`, `TestExprLocationTypedNil`, `TestExprLocationLongChains`, `TestExprLocationDeepChains`, `TestBoolExprArgListsAreNotShared`, `TestInvariantsOverCorpus`, `FuzzExprLocation`, `TestParseErrorPositionIsTheRefusedToken` |

| **Deliberate divergence.** `Integer` holds an `int64` (upstream's shape; PostgreSQL's `Integer` is `int`, 32 bits, and its scanner makes any larger literal a `Float`), and a minus over a numeric constant folds to an `Integer` whenever the negation fits `int64`, so int64's least value, which the scanner reads as a `Float` because its magnitude (9223372036854775808) does not fit, arrives as `Integer{-9223372036854775808}` in every radix and with underscores (`-9223372036854775808`, `-0x8000000000000000`, `-0o1000000000000000000000`, `-0b1` and 63 zeros, `-9_223_372_036_854_775_808`), parenthesised or not. PostgreSQL's raw tree keeps it a `Float` with the literal's text and its analysis (`make_const`) reads an int8. One past it, a positive magnitude, a decimal or an exponent stays a `Float`. Negating that `Integer` again (`- -9223372036854775808`) gives back a `Float` with the decimal text `9223372036854775808`, where PostgreSQL keeps the inner literal's text (`0x8000000000000000` for `- -0x8000000000000000`).
